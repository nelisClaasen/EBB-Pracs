\subsection{Comparison of PID Coefficients Between Design Methods}

Table \ref{tab:pid_comparison} summarises the PID coefficients obtained for $G_{11}$ and $G_{22}$ using both design methods.

\begin{table}[H]
    \centering
    \caption{Comparison of PID coefficients across design methods}
    \label{tab:pid_comparison}
    \begin{tblr}{colspec={Q[l]|Q[c]Q[c]|Q[c]Q[c]}, row{even}={bg=gray!20}}
        \toprule
        & \SetCell[c=2]{c} \textbf{$G_{11}$ (PWM1 $\to$ T1)} & & \SetCell[c=2]{c} \textbf{$G_{22}$ (PWM2 $\to$ T2)} & \\
        \textbf{Coefficient} & \textbf{Freq. Response} & \textbf{Root Locus} & \textbf{Freq. Response} & \textbf{Root Locus} \\
        \midrule
        $K_p$ & 0.0947 & 0.1527 & 0.1488 & 0.2328 \\
        $K_i$ & 0.00257 & 0.003479 & 0.00314 & 0.004226 \\
        $K_d$ & 0.6901 & 1.17921 & 1.174 & 1.90571 \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tblr}
\end{table}

A consistent pattern is visible across both plants: the root locus method produces larger $K_p$, $K_i$, and $K_d$ values than the frequency response method. Since $K_d = K_c$ and both $K_p = K_c(z_1+z_2)$ and $K_i = K_c z_1 z_2$ scale directly with $K_c$, this difference originates in how each method determines $K_c$. The root locus method enforces the magnitude condition at the complex design point $s_d$, where the plant has lower magnitude, requiring a larger $K_c$. The frequency response method enforces the same condition at the real frequency $\omega_s = 0.8\omega_{BW}$, where the plant magnitude is higher, so a smaller $K_c$ suffices. For $G_{22}$ this gives $K_c = 1.906$ (root locus) versus $K_c = 1.174$ (frequency response), a factor of $1.6\times$.

The D-action zeros also differ slightly between the two methods — $z_1 = 0.02674$ (frequency response) versus $z_1 = 0.02217$ (root locus) for $G_{22}$ — further amplifying the coefficient differences through the $K_p$ and $K_i$ expressions. Despite these numerical differences, both controller pairs met the design specifications and performed comparably in the physical implementation, confirming that the plant is robust to this level of gain variation between the two design methods.
