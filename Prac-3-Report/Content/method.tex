\subsection{PID Controller Design for \texorpdfstring{$G_{11}(s)$}{G11(s)}}

The design specifications are a $10\%$ overshoot and a rise time of $T_r = 96.1$ s. The damping ratio $\zeta$ is calculated as:
\begin{align*}
    \zeta &= \frac{-\ln(0.1)}{\sqrt{\pi^2 + [\ln(0.1)]^2}} = 0.591
\end{align*}
The required natural frequency $\omega_n$ is:
\begin{align*}
    \omega_n &= \frac{1.76(0.591)^3 - 0.417(0.591)^2 + 1.039(0.591) + 1}{96.1} = 0.01906 \text{ rad/s}
\end{align*}
The required closed-loop bandwidth $\omega_{BW}$ and phase margin $\Phi_M$ are:
\begin{align*}
    \omega_{BW} &= \omega_n \sqrt{(1-2\zeta^2)+\sqrt{4\zeta^4-4\zeta^2+2}} = 0.02211 \text{ rad/s} \\
    \Phi_M &= \tan^{-1} \left( \frac{2\zeta}{\sqrt{-2\zeta^2+\sqrt{1+4\zeta^4}}} \right) = 58.58^\circ
\end{align*}

Following the prescribed procedure, the PI controller zero is fixed at $z_2 = 0.1$, yielding $G_{PI}(s) = \frac{s + 0.1}{s}$. The new phase-margin frequency $\omega_s$ is selected as $80\%$ of the bandwidth:
\begin{align*}
    \omega_s &= 0.8 \times 0.02211 = 0.01769 \text{ rad/s}
\end{align*}

The target total loop phase at $\omega_s$ is:
\begin{align*}
    \phi_{required}(\omega_s) &= -180^\circ + 58.58^\circ = -121.42^\circ
\end{align*}
The phase contributions of the uncompensated plant (including dead time) and the PI controller at $\omega_s$ are:
\begin{align*}
    \phi_G(0.01769) &= -0.07^\circ - 66.77^\circ = -66.84^\circ \\
    \phi_{PI}(0.01769) &= -90^\circ + \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{0.01769}{0.1}\right) = -79.97^\circ
\end{align*}
The required phase addition from the D-action zero is:
\begin{align*}
    \phi_{add}(\omega_s) &= -121.42^\circ - (-66.84^\circ) - (-79.97^\circ) = +25.39^\circ
\end{align*}

The D-action zero $z_1$ is located to provide exactly $25.39^\circ$ of phase:
\begin{align*}
    \tan(25.39^\circ) &= \frac{0.01769}{z_1} \implies z_1 = 0.03727
\end{align*}
The controller gain $K_{cPID}$ is calculated to force unity magnitude at $\omega_s$:
\begin{align*}
    |G_{11}(j0.01769)| \times \frac{K_{cPID}|j0.01769 + 0.03727||j0.01769 + 0.1|}{|j0.01769|} &= 1 \\
    6.121 \times K_{cPID} \times \frac{0.04126 \times 0.10155}{0.01769} &= 1 \implies K_{cPID} = 0.6901
\end{align*}

Expanding the controller transfer function yields the parallel PID gains:
\begin{align*}
    G_{c11}(s) &= 0.6901 \frac{(s + 0.03727)(s + 0.1)}{s} = 0.6901s + 0.0947 + \frac{0.00257}{s}
\end{align*}
\textbf{Final Gains for \texorpdfstring{$G_{11}$}{G11}:} $K_p = 0.0947$, $K_i = 0.00257$, $K_d = 0.6901$

\subsection{PID Controller Design for \texorpdfstring{$G_{22}(s)$}{G22(s)}}

For a target rise time of $T_r = 122.55$ s and identical $10\%$ overshoot ($\zeta = 0.591$, $\Phi_M = 58.58^\circ$), the bandwidth requirements shift:
\begin{align*}
    \omega_n &= \frac{1.8316}{122.55} = 0.01495 \text{ rad/s} \\
    \omega_{BW} &= 0.01495 \times 1.160 = 0.01734 \text{ rad/s}
\end{align*}

With $z_2 = 0.1$, the new phase-margin frequency is:
\begin{align*}
    \omega_s &= 0.8 \times 0.01734 = 0.01387 \text{ rad/s}
\end{align*}

The required total loop phase is identical: $\phi_{required} = -121.42^\circ$. Evaluating the $G_{22}$ plant and PI controller at the new $\omega_s$:
\begin{align*}
    \phi_G(0.01387) &= -66.61^\circ (\text{Core}) - 0.12^\circ (\text{Delay}) = -66.73^\circ \\
    \phi_{PI}(0.01387) &= -90^\circ + \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{0.01387}{0.1}\right) = -82.10^\circ
\end{align*}
The required phase addition is:
\begin{align*}
    \phi_{add}(\omega_s) &= -121.42^\circ - (-66.73^\circ) - (-82.10^\circ) = +27.41^\circ
\end{align*}

The D-action zero $z_1$ is located to provide $27.41^\circ$ of phase:
\begin{align*}
    \tan(27.41^\circ) &= \frac{0.01387}{z_1} \implies z_1 = 0.02674
\end{align*}
The overall controller gain $K_{cPID}$ is found via unity magnitude mapping:
\begin{align*}
    |G_{22}(j0.01387)| \times \frac{K_{cPID}|j0.01387 + 0.02674||j0.01387 + 0.1|}{|j0.01387|} &= 1 \\
    3.885 \times K_{cPID} \times \frac{0.03013 \times 0.10096}{0.01387} &= 1 \implies K_{cPID} = 1.174
\end{align*}

Expanding the controller polynomial yields:
\begin{align*}
    G_{c22}(s) &= 1.174 \frac{(s + 0.02674)(s + 0.1)}{s} = 1.174s + 0.1488 + \frac{0.00314}{s}
\end{align*}
\textbf{Final Gains for \texorpdfstring{$G_{22}$}{G22}:} $K_p = 0.1488$, $K_i = 0.00314$, $K_d = 1.174$

\subsection{Root Locus Technique for G11}
    
For the transfer function $G_{11}$ using equation [\ref{eq:first_order_Tr}] the current rise time was found to be $\approx 289.78~\mathrm{sec}$. Therefore, the target rise time is $96.593~\mathrm{sec}$.

Then using equations [\ref{eq:zeta_from_OS}] and [\ref{eq:omega_n_from_zeta_and_Tr}] it was found that:
\begin{align*}
    \zeta &\approx 0.5912 \\
    \omega_n &\approx 0.018967
\end{align*}

The design point was then found using [\ref{eq:sigma_d}] and [\ref{eq:omega_d}] giving $s_d = -0.01121 + j0.0153$.

The second form of the PID equation [\ref{eq:PID_TF}] was then used to find the controller transfer function.

Furthermore, the Pad\'e approximation [\ref{eq:Pade_approx}] was used to find an approximation of the transfer function's dead time. The new plant transfer function was then found to be:

\begin{equation*}
    G_p(s) = \frac{(1-0.0725s)}{(1+0.0725s)}\frac{0.1175}{(s+0.007592)}
\end{equation*}

Letting $z_2 = 0.1$ the angles of the poles and zeros to the design point, and [\ref{eq:root_locus_angle_condition}], it was found:

\begin{align*}
    \theta_{z=z_1} &+ \theta_{z=z_2} + \theta_{z=13.793} - (\theta_{p=0} + \theta_{p=-13.793} + \theta_{p=-0.007592}) = \pm 180^\circ\\
    \theta_{z=z_1} &\approx -180^\circ + 219.90^\circ \\
    \theta_{z=z_1} &\approx 39.905^\circ
\end{align*}

Therefore, the value of $z_1$ was found as:
\begin{align*}
    \tan(\theta_{z=z_1}) &= \frac{0.0153}{z_1 - 0.01121} \\
    z_1 &= \frac{0.0153 + 0.01121 \tan(\theta_{z=z_1})}{\tan(\theta_{z=z_1})} \\
    z_1 &\approx 0.0295055
\end{align*}

The controller gain $K_c$ was found using [\ref{eq:root_locus_mag_condition}] where:
\begin{align*}
    |K_c G_c(s_d)G_p(s_d)| &= 1\\
    |G_c(s_d)G_p(s_d)| &\approx 0.848
\end{align*}

Therefore:
\begin{align*}
    K_c = \frac{1}{|G_c(s_d)G_p(s_d)|} \approx 1.17921
\end{align*}

The final controller transfer function was then found to be:
\begin{align*}
    G_c(s) = \frac{1.17921 (s + 0.1)(s+0.0295055)}{s}
\end{align*}

Using [\ref{eq:Kd_from_Kc}], [\ref{eq:Ki_from_Kc}] and [\ref{eq:Kp_from_Kc}] the PID values where found to be:

\begin{align*}
    K_p &= K_c (z_1 + z_2) = 0.152714 \\
    K_i &= K_c (z_1 z_2) = 0.003479 \\
    K_d &= K_c = 1.17921
\end{align*}

In order to confirm the design, the root locus was made using matlab, where the root locus plot has an intersection very close to the design point.

\subsection{Root Locus Technique for G22}

For the transfer function $G_{22}$ using equation [\ref{eq:first_order_Tr}] the current rise time was found to be $\approx 367.65~\mathrm{sec}$. Therefore, the target rise time is $122.55~\mathrm{sec}$.

Then using equations [\ref{eq:zeta_from_OS}] and [\ref{eq:omega_n_from_zeta_and_Tr}] it was found that:
\begin{align*}
    \zeta &\approx 0.5912 \\
    \omega_n &\approx 0.01495
\end{align*}

The design point was then found using [\ref{eq:sigma_d}] and [\ref{eq:omega_d}] giving $s_d = -0.008838 + j0.0121$.

The second form of the PID equation [\ref{eq:PID_TF}] was then used to find the controller transfer function.

Furthermore, the Pad\'e approximation [\ref{eq:Pade_approx}] was used to find an approximation of the transfer function's dead time. The new plant transfer function was then found to be:

\begin{equation*}
    G_p(s) = \frac{(1-0.0725s)}{(1+0.0725s)} \frac{0.05872}{(s+0.005984)}
\end{equation*}

Letting $z_2 = 0.1$ the angles of the poles and zeros to the design point, and [\ref{eq:root_locus_angle_condition}], it was found:

\begin{align*}
    \theta_{z=z_1} &+ \theta_{z=z_2} + \theta_{z=13.793} - (\theta_{p=0} + \theta_{p=-13.793} + \theta_{p=-0.005984}) = \pm 180^\circ\\
    \theta_{z=z_1} &\approx -180^\circ + 222.12^\circ \\
    \theta_{z=z_1} &\approx 42.119^\circ
\end{align*}

Therefore, the value of $z_1$ was found as:
\begin{align*}
    \tan(\theta_{z=z_1}) &= \frac{0.0121}{z_1 - 0.00884} \\
    z_1 &= \frac{0.0121 + 0.00884 \tan(\theta_{z=z_1})}{\tan(\theta_{z=z_1})} \\
    z_1 &\approx 0.0221733
\end{align*}

The controller gain $K_c$ was found using [\ref{eq:root_locus_mag_condition}] where:
\begin{align*}
    |K_c G_c(s_d)G_p(s_d)| &= 1\\
    |G_c(s_d)G_p(s_d)| &\approx 0.52473
\end{align*}

Therefore:
\begin{align*}
    K_c = \frac{1}{|G_c(s_d)G_p(s_d)|} \approx 1.90571
\end{align*}

The final controller transfer function was then found to be:
\begin{align*}
    G_c(s) = \frac{1.90571 (s + 0.1)(s+0.0221733)}{s}
\end{align*}

Using [\ref{eq:Kd_from_Kc}], [\ref{eq:Ki_from_Kc}] and [\ref{eq:Kp_from_Kc}] the PID values where found to be:

\begin{align*}
    K_p &= K_c (z_1 + z_2) = 0.232827 \\
    K_i &= K_c (z_1 z_2) = 0.0042256 \\
    K_d &= K_c = 1.90571
\end{align*}

In order to confirm the design, the root locus was made using matlab, where the root locus plot has an intersection very close to the design point.
